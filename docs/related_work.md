# Related work and claim boundary

## Scope and method

This is a targeted claim audit, not an exhaustive or systematic review. The search was last updated **2026-10-01**. It used title, DOI, publisher/conference, arXiv, and topic searches across learned physical simulation, topology-changing fracture, stochastic event prediction, uncertainty calibration, peptidoglycan (PG) mechanics, and 2025--2026 work. Technical statements below are grounded in primary papers or their publisher/conference records; reviews were used only to discover candidates. A preprint is labeled as such. “Unknown” means the searched primary record did not establish the item, not that it does not exist.

Three distinctions control the comparison:

1. A proximity graph changing as particles move, or an adaptive computational mesh changing resolution, is not material-bond deletion.
2. A scalar damage/phase field or crack-tip graph can represent fracture without predicting the persistent chemical network required here.
3. A softmax score or ensemble spread is not, by itself, demonstrated calibration of a stochastic event distribution.

## What is already established

General graph simulators already support long autoregressive physical rollouts, mesh-based mechanics, constraint-based correction, and discontinuous rigid contact [@sanchez_gonzalez2020gns; @pfaff2021meshgraphnets; @rubanova2022constraint; @allen2023contact]. Hybrid stochastic residual learning also predates this project [@ajay2018stochastic]. Therefore, neither “a GNN simulator,” “discontinuous dynamics,” nor “a stochastic learned residual” is a contribution boundary.

Learned fracture with graph state and real topology change is also prior art. Karapiperis and Kochmann store a new graph after each failed beam and predict a distribution over the next intact beam [@karapiperis2023fracture]. Perera, Guzzetti, and Agrawal predict crack coalescence and propagation from crack-tip graphs [@perera2022coalescence]; ACCURATE then transfers that family across crack geometry, domain, and tensile/shear loading changes using small fine-tuning sets [@perera2023accurate]. Later graph mechanics work predicts damage, crack growth, or coalescence using phase-field or peridynamic reference simulations [@perera2023adaptive; @perera2024multiscale; @zhou2024mpnn; @feng2025coalescence; @feng2025phasefield; @feng2026plastic]. These are direct overlap, not merely background. The two Perera--Agrawal adaptive-mesh models change numerical discretization while representing a diffuse physical crack; they do not establish persistent material-edge deletion.

Close non-GNN precedents also constrain evaluation. StressNet combines convolutional damage-history features and recurrent stress histories to predict maximum stress through HOSS fracture sequences [@wang2021stressnet]. Font-Clos et al. use support-vector and image-based deep models to predict first failure, rupture strain/location, and full crack paths from undeformed 2D silica structure, including transfer across sample size and application to experimental images [@font_clos2022silica]. Neither predicts an autoregressive bond-event/mechanics state, but both are relevant structure/history-only failure baselines.

Recent work further narrows the claim. Static network topology can predict local failure thresholds in disordered silica [@bachhav2025list]; a GNS can roll out frictional earthquake rupture fields and test several out-of-distribution (OOD) controls [@liu2025earthquake]; and a physics-informed GNN can predict deformation in disordered fibrous networks [@yang2026fibrous]. The Hu et al. bond-associated peridynamic surrogate is especially relevant to edge-centered mechanics, but it remains an arXiv preprint in this audit and its record carries an arXiv overlap notice [@hu2024peridynamic].

Uncertainty remains a separable requirement. Most mechanics papers in the matrix are deterministic. Ajay et al. model stochastic residuals, while Karapiperis and Kochmann output a next-edge probability; neither establishes calibrated PG rupture probabilities. Graph-specific conformal methods demonstrate coverage machinery on non-mechanics graph tasks [@zhang2025conformal], and proper scoring rules provide the evaluation principle for probabilistic predictions [@gneiting2007proper]. They are methodological baselines, not evidence that the proposed model is calibrated.

PG mechanics and structure are also established independently of learned surrogates. Experimental work measures hydrated sacculus elasticity, live-cell stress stiffening/turgor, and glycan-chain architecture [@yao1999elasticity; @deng2011stress; @turner2018imaging]. Atomic and coarse-grained simulations connect molecular structure to mechanics and growth [@gumbart2014atomic; @furchtgott2011shape; @nguyen2015growth]. These works constrain geometry, loading, and experimental comparisons; simulated fixtures cannot replace them.

## Baseline and evaluation consequences

At minimum, a future evaluation should include: a no-event or persistence model; the inherited mechanistic simulator; a deterministic GNS-style rollout; an explicit event-classifier/next-edge baseline motivated by Karapiperis and Kochmann; a structure-only failure/path baseline motivated by Font-Clos et al.; and physics-structured fracture comparators appropriate to the available representation. ACCURATE is a transfer/generalization comparator when fine-tuning data are allowed, while StressNet motivates a damage-history-to-stress ablation rather than a topology rollout [@perera2023accurate; @wang2021stressnet; @font_clos2022silica]. Each receives the same information, split, trajectory budget, and inference horizon. Preprocessing statistics must be fit on training groups only. Adaptive-mesh papers are comparators for field prediction, not evidence that material topology is handled [@perera2023adaptive; @perera2024multiscale].

Event uncertainty must be evaluated separately from state error. Report a proper score such as negative log likelihood or Brier score, reliability/calibration diagnostics, event precision/recall and timing, and rollout-level distributional coverage where the assumptions are defensible [@gneiting2007proper; @zhang2025conformal]. A next-edge softmax is not called calibrated without these tests. OOD claims require predefined shifts in network size, topology, PG chemistry/strand statistics, loading, or material parameters; interpolation on a random trajectory split is not OOD.

Experimental evidence must remain a separate gate. PG architecture, elasticity, and live-cell/sacculus measurements can constrain or falsify simulation outcomes [@yao1999elasticity; @deng2011stress; @turner2018imaging], but they do not become training or validation evidence merely by being rendered alongside a simulation. Applying a simulation-trained feature model to an experimental structure without experimental fracture outcomes, as in the silica transfer case, is also not experimental failure validation [@font_clos2022silica]. Grouped biological repeats, protocol provenance, and uncertainty are required.

## Conditional contribution boundary

No novelty claim should rest on generic message passing, graph-based physics simulation, learned fracture, adaptive meshes, peridynamic graph surrogates, an edge-failure classifier, or an attractive 3D rendering. Those components already have close precedents.

A defensible contribution is conditional on **demonstrating all of the following together**:

- PG-specific joint rollouts of mechanics and irreversible bond-event/topology evolution, without future-state leakage;
- controlled, persistent topology changes with physically meaningful PG state and loading coordinates;
- predefined OOD evaluation against matched mechanistic and learned baselines;
- calibrated stochastic event/state uncertainty assessed with proper scores, not confidence language alone;
- independent experimental grounding in real PG/cell-wall structure or mechanics, with negative/null findings retained; and
- a scientist-facing workflow that exposes trained model provenance, uncertainty, comparison data, and limitations rather than only visualization.

Even if all six are achieved, the eventual manuscript should make a scoped contribution claim and avoid “first,” “unique,” or “state of the art” language until the search is refreshed and an independent claim review is complete.

## Search limitations

- Search coverage ends on 2026-10-01 and may miss newly indexed, non-English, poorly indexed, or terminology-mismatched work.
- Publisher pages and openly accessible manuscripts were used; no paywall, credential, or private-data restriction was bypassed.
- Full baseline and artifact inventories were not recoverable from every accessible primary record; these cells are marked **unknown**.
- The Hu et al. record is a preprint and includes an arXiv administrative overlap note; it is not treated as peer-reviewed evidence.
- Code/data links were recorded only when the primary record or clearly identified author repository established them. Availability does not imply that artifacts were executed or reproduced in this task.
- This audit evaluates claim overlap. It does not validate this repository's simulator, model, experiments, or scientific conclusions.
